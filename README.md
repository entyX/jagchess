# JagChess

## Inspiration

Chess clubs do far more than play games, but much of that work still happens across group chats, spreadsheets, email lists, and disconnected chess platforms. I wanted to give clubs a dedicated digital home that feels as engaging as chess itself: a place where organizers can run the club and members have a reason to keep coming back.

## What it does

JagChess is an all-in-one, branded platform for chess clubs, academies, federations, and online communities. It brings together puzzles, events, attendance, learning paths, newsletters, leaderboards, rewards, and member roles in one hub. Members can earn points and build streaks, while organizers can manage the club without juggling multiple tools.

## How I built it

I built JagChess around a modular, no-code experience. Organizers can turn features on or off, arrange page blocks, set their club color, and create a branded home that reflects their own community. I designed the core experience so that modules share the same members, permissions, and points system, keeping the platform cohesive instead of fragmented.

## Challenges I ran into

The biggest challenge was balancing flexibility with simplicity. A chess federation and a small school club need very different tools, but neither should face a complicated setup process. I addressed that by making the platform modular: clubs can begin with essentials such as puzzles and events, then add features as their programming grows.

## Accomplishments that I'm proud of

I'm proud that JagChess turns routine club administration into a member experience. Puzzles, attendance, leaderboards, streaks, and rewards all feed into the same points system, making participation visible and motivating. I'm also proud of the customization flow: a club can create a branded, shareable home without needing to build a website or manage a stack of separate products.

## What I learned

I learned that club software succeeds when it supports both sides of a community: the organizer who needs clarity and the member who needs momentum. I also learned that gamification works best when it reinforces meaningful actions, such as showing up, learning, and solving puzzles, rather than existing as a separate feature.

## What's next for JagChess

Next, I want to deepen the tools that help clubs measure progress and support members over time. That includes richer coach dashboards, more flexible learning paths, stronger federation-level management, and integrations that let clubs connect their existing chess accounts while keeping JagChess as their central community hub.
